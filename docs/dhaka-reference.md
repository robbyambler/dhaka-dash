# Dhaka visual reference (for painting scenery in code)

Source: 43 Wikimedia Commons photos in `reference/photos/` (credits in `reference/CREDITS.md`). File names below are the photos each section was read from. Hex values are eyeballed from those photos, then nudged toward pixel-art friendly saturation. Photos of: Shahbagh landmarks (National Museum), Hatirjheel water-taxi, and a running metro train on the viaduct were NOT found (see end). Those parts come from general knowledge and are marked (K).

## Global Dhaka rules (apply to every level)

- Sky is almost never clean blue: it is milky grey-white haze (#C9CDD2 to #D8D6CF), even on "clear" days. Pure blue (#4E8FD6) only at top of frame in a good morning.
- Road asphalt is mid-dark grey with a warm tint (#4A4A4C), faded white dashed lines (#D9D6CC). Footpaths are rust-brown paver tiles (#8C5A47) with a yellow tactile strip (#C9A12E) down the middle.
- Kerbs and median edges: black-and-white painted blocks (#1E1E22 / #DADAD6), alternating about every 1 tile. Very Dhaka. Use on every median.
- Overhead: bundles of black cables sagging between poles (dark #1B1B1E, 3-6 lines, droop in a smooth curve), concrete poles with a tangle at the top.
- Buildings: dirty concrete in cream, ochre, pale green, faded pink; stained streaks, AC boxes hanging on walls, grilled windows (dark grids), rooftop water tanks (black/blue), billboards stacked on roofs, bamboo scaffolding on unfinished towers (K, seen as green scaffold net at Karwan Bazar).
- Trees: heavy dark-green canopies (#2F5A2A, #4E7F32, highlights #8CB33F), often overhanging the footpath. Flame tree (krishnochura) blossoms: small orange-red dots (#D9441E) on a few trees.
- Crowd colors: lungi checks (green/yellow/blue stripes), white punjabi, hijab/burqa in olive (#9BA06A), black, teal; backpacks. Students everywhere.
- Rickshaws are the color confetti in every scene. Even 1-2 px of red/green/yellow/pink on a hood sells it.
- Fences: white painted spear-top steel fence along footpath with clipped hedge in front (seen Dhanmondi/Shahbagh street, `v-cng-street-scene*.jpg`). Steel pedestrian guard-rail with vertical bars along footpath edge (Farmgate).

# Vehicles

## Pedal rickshaw (cycle rickshaw)
Photos: `v-rickshaw-art-back.jpg`, `v-rickshaw-bangladeshi-style.jpg`, `v-rickshaw-art-b.jpg`, `v-rickshaw-dhaka-a.jpg`, `l04-farmgate-metro-overbridge.jpg` (in traffic)

- Body shape: bicycle in front (big wheels ~26", thin black frame), driver sits high on a saddle. Behind: a passenger bench with a half-circle folding hood. Two small rear wheels; the platform is a low metal tray with shaped back panel. Whole thing is 1.5 tiles long x about 1 tile high; the hood is the highest part and the most colourful.
- Proportions: wheel diameter = 0.55 of total height; hood height = 1.2x the driver's head; passenger seat width = about 1.5 persons.
- Colours: hood outer panel is in black / dark navy with gold sequin edging (#C9A227) and bright discs (red #E0212F, pink #F26BA3, teal #12A88F, blue #2D63C6, yellow #F5C518, white). Back panel (tin plate, silver #BFC2C4) painted with: tiger heads in yellow #F2A900/orange, birds (peacock, parrot, dove) in white/blue/pink, lotus flowers (pink + red), hearts (red with yellow outline), Bengali lettering. Mudguards: shiny chrome with painted flowers. Frame bars: striped candy bands (red/green/blue/white/orange). Fringe: tassels (yellow, pink, blue) and little Bangladesh flags (green #006A4E with red circle #F42A41) on the handlebars.
- Hood variants to randomise: (a) red+gold sequin with white flowers, (b) green with yellow circle motifs, (c) blue with pink, (d) black with rainbow rosettes, (e) plain plastic-sheet hood for rainy levels (blue/red tarp).
- Art patterns: concentric circles (rosettes) in alternating 2-3 colours, teardrop/paisley (red/white), 5-point stars, hearts, fans of leaves, tiger, bird pairs facing each other on rear skirt.
- Back text (real examples seen): "মাছে ভাতে বাঙ্গালী" ("Bengali by fish and rice"), "মা", "তানজিলা" (a name). Common messages: "মায়ের দোয়া" (mother's blessings), "আল্লাহ ভরসা" (trust in God), "ভালোবাসা", "মা বাবার আশীর্বাদ".
- Driver: lungi or trousers, shirt, sandals, headscarf/gamchha on the head, reclined legs; passengers: women in saris/burqa, men with bags, students.

## CNG auto-rickshaw (green baby-taxi)
Photos: `v-cng-green.jpg` (rear view), `v-cng-street-scene.jpg`, `v-cng-street-scene-b.jpg`, `l05-karwan-bazar-a.jpg`, `l07-mohakhali-rail-gate.jpg`

- Colour: body deep green #2E7D3E (Dhaka CNG is bottle-green, with bright #3FA34D highlights), black fabric canopy/roof #1B1B1D, bright yellow stripe on some; some Dhaka CNGs have a thin black lower stripe. Roof is a rounded rectangle, black vinyl, with a small rear window. Number plate green #0D8F5A with white text.
- Shape: tiny cab, 1.5 tiles long and ~1 tile high; 3 wheels (one front, two rear); front is a rounded nose with a single headlight and a black grille (diagonal/vertical bars); rear is square with a spare tyre/rear bumper. Metal cage: both sides are covered with a black steel mesh/grille from waist to roof (the "cage") - draw as a dark dotted/hatched pattern over the side. Passengers sit visible through the mesh. Driver sits at the left in front, no door.
- Details: yellow Bengali text on rear (company/fare slogan), metal bumper, side mirrors on bars, jerry cans / loads tied to the back (rural), sometimes bright decals. It sounds like a buzzing two-stroke: yellow-orange exhaust puff.
- Street size: about the same as a rickshaw but boxier and lower than a bus; very common (4-8 per scene).

## Top details that make it unmistakably Dhaka
1. Rickshaws: huge colourful hood + painted tin back with Bengali slogans, tiger/bird/flower art, tassels. 3-5 per screen at all times.
2. Black-and-white kerbs on every median plus the dark overhead cable tangle.
3. Battered buses: peeling paint scrapes in bright yellow/green/red, open door with a hanger-on, lit route board in Bengali.
4. Green caged CNG auto-rickshaws everywhere (bottle-green, black mesh sides, black roof).
5. Milky haze sky + crowd on the road: pedestrians, vendors and people standing on the rail track; stacks of signboards on every wall in Bengali script.
